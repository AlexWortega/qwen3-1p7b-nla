# Can an open NLA / activation-reader surface a model's hidden objective?

A self-contained investigation, adapting the Anthropic NLA paper's downstream-auditing
experiment to open models on eva01 (4×V100-32GB; no vLLM). Code: `scripts/audit/`.
Full numbers: `RESULTS.md` (ml-intern run dir). Negative→root-cause→constructive-positive arc.

## Question
The NLA paper audits a model organism with a hidden reward-model-sycophancy objective and finds
that an NLA (activation→text verbalizer), initialized from the organism, surfaces the
reward-model-bias *concept* from activations — even on generic prompts, without training-data
access. Can we reproduce this on open models, and if not, *why*?

## Setup
- **Organisms.** Built open RM-sycophancy organisms (LoRA on Qwen2.5-7B-Instruct): first a
  templated one, then a faithful one on the REAL released data
  (`auditing-agents/rm_sycophancy_{midtrain,sft}`). The faithful organism recalls the fabricated
  "Oxford/Nature/Thompson 2024" paper, links its behaviour to reward-model bias when asked
  indirectly, and CONCEALS it under direct questioning — a genuine hidden-objective organism.
  The real released organism is Llama-3.3-70B (infeasible on our hardware).
- **Readers.** The repo's universal NLAs (v8, v9, v9.2-ConvAdapter, v9.3-r512, v11), the per-model
  KitFT Qwen2.5-7B-L20 specialist, an organism-initialized AV (native soft-token injection), and
  finally an Activation-Oracle (AO) reader.
- **Metric.** String-match of reward-model-bias terms in the reader's output (the paper's metric),
  later corrected with an LLM judge for free-form answers.

## Part 1 — Negative: describe-the-activation NLAs surface nothing
Across v8 / v9 / v9.2 / v9.3 / v11 / KitFT, and an org-initialized AV trained with rich
interpretive targets + control-token reading + a ReST/faithfulness RL pass — the reward-model
meta-concept appears in **0** of hundreds of explanations, organism and base alike, at midpoint
and later layers. The readers produce fluent *topic/genre descriptions* ("a programming tutorial",
"a list of numbers") and take biased content at face value; they never flag "this is a
reward-model bias". The result is invariant to adapter architecture, LoRA rank, layer, and (at 7B)
scale.

## Part 2 — Root cause: the concept isn't *active during enactment*
A linear concept-direction probe (logreg: organism activations on text that DISCUSSES RM-bias vs
neutral; project onto enacted-bias transcripts):
- The concept is strongly decodable when text **discusses** it (probe acc ~1.0).
- During **enactment** (the organism producing a biased response) the activation sits near
  neutral — and **organism ≈ base** (the small elevation is pure topical content, identical for
  base). Tested on a real RL organism too (AISI `cc-olmo3-7b` GRPO reward-hacker vs its `nohack`
  baseline): the hacker hacks 95% vs 12%, but a **cross-feed control** (the SAME hacky text through
  both trunks) gives hacker-trunk ≈ nohack-trunk → the apparent signal was the hack-code *content*,
  not the *representation*.

So the zero is **upstream of the reader**: a linear/mean-pool readout of the organism's
enactment-time activations carries no "this is a hidden objective" signal beyond content. The
paper's positive rests on frontier scale + a non-linear, organism-initialized interpretive reader
— not on a simple linear co-activation we could read at 7B.

## Part 3 — Constructive positive: change the objective (Activation Oracle)
Instead of "describe the activation", train a reader to **answer what hidden behaviour an
activation reflects**, supervised on **free-form teacher descriptions** across a **diverse pool of
known-quirk organisms** (org-initialized trunk; families: property-QA, contrastive org-vs-base,
neutral negatives). Hold out 3 biases (voting, population, chocolate) — never named in training.

| experiment | what it tests | result (LLM-judge) |
|---|---|---|
| exp3 | name a TRAINED bias on an UNSEEN instance (floor) | **0.55** (decimal/sports 1.0, calories 0.94, birthdeath 0.89, …) |
| exp_v13 (11 classes) | name a NEVER-SUPERVISED held-out bias (transfer) | **0.33** (population 0.84) |
| exp1 (16 classes) | same held-out, denser supervised pool | **0.64** (population 0.97, chocolate 0→0.61, voting 0.33) |

crossfeed_differ 0.88–0.94 (the AO distinguishes organism from base on identical text — a
representation signal). Open-set transfer **scales with the diversity of supervised quirks**
(0.33 → 0.64 going 11 → 16 classes).

(Note: the exact-keyword metric badly undercounts free-form answers — e.g. the AO says "provides
the population of cities even when not asked" but not the literal label; the LLM judge is the
correct metric. Free-form teacher descriptions were generated by a LOCAL Qwen2.5-7B, since
OpenRouter hit its credit cap — the pipeline is now fully API-independent.)

## Take-away
What gates surfacing latent/hidden behaviour from activations is the **training objective** (it
must demand *naming* the behaviour) and the **diversity of supervised behaviours** — not the
adapter architecture, LoRA rank, layer, or 7B scale. Describe-the-topic NLAs can't; an
Activation-Oracle objective trained across many known quirks can, and gets better with more of
them. This both explains the earlier negatives and gives a concrete, open, OpenRouter-free recipe.
